OpenFinance lets an AI place real orders and move funds between your own wallets, so the design keeps the important decisions with you. The wallet is yours, nothing is sent until you confirm, an agent can't send your money to anyone else, and you decide where your keys go. The sections below take each of those in turn, starting with the money itself.

## Who holds your funds

Your wallet is **self-custodial and embedded**. It is created when you sign up and is powered by Privy.

- You get one EVM address, the same on every EVM chain, and one Solana address.
- The same wallet is used in the OpenFinance app and over MCP.
- You can export the private keys at any time from **Settings**, under **Export Keys**, and use the wallet elsewhere. Exporting needs two-factor authentication and a code from your authenticator app.

Funds you deposit to a venue sit at that venue under your account: USDC on Hyperliquid, pUSD in your Polymarket deposit wallet. Options positions sit in your own broker account.

## Confirmation before every action

The AI agent confirms every order and action with you before it acts: trades, transfers, bridges, withdrawals and token launches. We recommend you always keep this step and use it.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="In the OpenFinance app">
    Before an action that can move funds or place an order, the agent shows you a summary with the details and waits for you to reply **confirm**. Nothing is placed until you confirm.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="From your own AI agent">
    OpenFinance tells your agent to show you a summary (what, how much, where) and wait for you to reply "confirm" in the current conversation. Your AI client can add a second check by asking you to approve each tool call. Keep that on, and do not add OpenFinance tools to an auto-approve list.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Warning>
Whether an outside agent follows its instructions depends on the model and the client. Stay in the loop for anything that moves money, and read the summary before you reply "confirm". Onchain transactions cannot be reversed.
</Warning>

## Where money can go

**An agent or API key can only move money between your own wallets.** Your own wallets are your EVM address, your Solana address and your Polymarket deposit wallet. The server checks every payout before anything is signed and refuses any other address, so a fooled agent or a leaked key cannot send your funds to another address.

- **Transfers, bridges and swaps** from an agent must pay one of your own wallets. This covers `onchain_send_token`, Relay bridges and swaps, Jupiter swaps and Polymarket withdrawals.
- **Hyperliquid withdrawals go to your own address on Arbitrum.**
- **Sending to someone else happens only on the [Wallet](/product/wallet) page.** It needs a code from your authenticator app. Set one up first in **Settings**, under **Security**, **Two-factor authentication**.
- **One code approves one outside transfer.** It lasts at most 15 minutes and cannot be reused.

This stops transfers to other addresses. It does not judge trades: an agent or a leaked key can still place a bad trade in your account, including buying a token or pool that someone else controls, which moves value to them. Keep confirmations on and fund only what you are willing to risk.

## Other checks
- **Thin or volatile tokens are flagged** before a swap, with market cap, liquidity and the one-day move.
- **Launches are simulated or previewed first**, because they cannot be undone.

## What an API key can do

An `open_` API key acts as your account. Anyone who has it can read your balances, place trades, move funds between your own wallets and call models on your credits. It cannot send funds to anyone else's address.

- Prefer OAuth sign-in over a key where your client supports it.
- Keep a key in an environment variable or your client's secret prompt. Never commit it. Never paste it into a chat, an issue or a screenshot.
- Never build it into a browser or mobile app. Some Mainframe trading templates ask a visitor to paste their own key into the open tab at runtime; only do that on an app you deployed yourself and trust.
- If you think a key has leaked, click **Rotate key** on [External AI](/product/external-ai) straight away. The old key stops working at once. **Revoke** switches it off and disconnects every connected AI app.
- Turn on two-factor under **Settings > Security**. It is needed to send to anyone else and to export your wallet keys.
- Approve an OAuth consent page only for a connection you started yourself.

See [Authentication](/mcp/authentication).

## Identity checks and venue rules

OpenFinance itself does not run KYC. That does not remove the rules of the venues and providers you use:

- **Polymarket** features are not available where Polymarket itself is not available.
- **Card and bank onramps** are run by Moonpay and Onramp.money, which may require an identity check.
- **Options** trade in your own IBKR or Robinhood account, under that broker's checks and permissions.
- You are responsible for following the laws that apply where you live.

## Protect yourself from bad instructions

An agent with trading access will act on text it reads. Treat that as a risk.

- Do not ask an agent to act on instructions from a web page, an email or a document you do not trust.
- Never let it take an address, a token or an amount from that kind of content without checking it yourself.
- A token's name or description is not a reason to trust it. Verify the contract address.

## Risk

Trading crypto, perps, prediction markets and new tokens carries a high risk of loss. Leverage can lose your whole margin, and prediction shares and new tokens can go to zero. AI models make mistakes. Nothing in these docs or in an agent's replies is financial advice.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can my agent trade without asking me?">
    No. The AI agent confirms every order and action with you before it acts: it shows a summary and waits for you to reply "confirm". With an outside client, also keep its tool-approval prompts on as a second check.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can my agent send money to someone else?">
    No. An agent or API key can only move funds between your own wallets, and the server refuses any other address. To send to another person, use the [Wallet](/product/wallet) page with a code from your authenticator app.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is there a practice mode?">
    No. You can ask the agent to simulate or quote a trade without placing it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I take my wallet somewhere else?">
    Yes. Turn on two-factor under Settings, Security, then export the EVM key or the Solana key from Settings, under Export Keys. Keep exported keys offline and private.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I report a security problem?">
    Use the private "Report a vulnerability" option on the Security tab of the [OpenFinance MCP repository](https://github.com/openfinance-tech/mcp). Do not open a public issue.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
